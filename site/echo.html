Study · the Echo Index

How much crypto "consensus" is one voice repeated?

A narrative agent trusts agreement between voices. Agreement only carries information if the voices are independent. So we measured it, on real public posts, with exactly the echo check Hanko's decision engine runs. The answer was not the one we expected, and the study found a bug in Hanko before it found anything else.

The result

What it found in Hanko first

Before this study, Hanko's echo detector missed every one of the copies below. Channels append their own boilerplate (an ad, a "News | Markets | YouTube" footer), and every added word dilutes a Jaccard similarity score. A verbatim headline scored 0.53 against the 0.6 line, so one headline counted as two voices. The fix (D-14) also compares containment, which padding cannot dilute, and can only ever demote more voices, never fewer. The regression test is built on two of these real posts.

    What it does not show

    • –Rewrites of one press release still count as separate voices. The Swiss spot Bitcoin ETF approval was covered by several of these channels in their own words within hours. For opinions that rule is right; for news it is generous, so echo inflation here is if anything undercounted.
    • –Naming the same token is not converging on the same narrative. In a six-hour window, two channels mentioning BTC are usually telling two different stories.
    • –Fifteen Telegram news channels over fourteen days, not X influencers. Copy-paste shilling on X may look very different. This measures one corner of the market, and says so.
